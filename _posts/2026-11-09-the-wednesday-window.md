The week opened with SPX at 7,568.92 and VIX at 20.83 — the kind of elevated vol that sits on a tape when the market is waiting for something it cannot price precisely. Part of that 20.83 was structural, the residue of positioning from an already-stressed October. Part of it was calendar premium: the midterm elections were on Tuesday, and when you can't know the result, the options market charges for the uncertainty. By Wednesday morning, the uncertainty was gone.

Wednesday's session ran the script. SPX climbed 119.70 handles to 7,688.62. VIX collapsed from 20.83 to 16.61 — a 4.22-point compression in a single session, touching an intraday low of 16.57. Looking at Wednesday's close, the read was hard to avoid: the market had absorbed an overhanging event, vol had normalized, and whatever stress had lingered from October appeared to have cleared. Twenty-four hours later, that read cost you dearly.

## The compression was arithmetic, not regime

**When an election result lands, the vol that was pricing it disappears mechanically.** Option sellers who had demanded extra premium to carry exposure through an uncertain event no longer face that uncertainty — the event has occurred. They sell that premium, implied vol falls, and the VIX drops sharply. That's what happened between Tuesday's close and Wednesday's close: not a regime reset, not a change in dealer net positioning, not a new structural bid for the index. The calendar had simply removed one source of premium from the options surface, and the compression reflected it perfectly — 4.22 points, gone.

What doesn't change when the election clears is the underlying gamma positioning. If dealers entered the week net short gamma — forced to hedge by chasing price in either direction — that condition doesn't expire with the ballot count. It persists until the options that created it are rolled, exercised, or hedged away. Wednesday's surface compression was real. Wednesday's regime change was not.

## Thursday confirmed what the compression had hidden

**Within ninety minutes of Thursday's open, VIX had already spiked above 28.** The intraday move was the largest single-day VIX gain since April 2026 — eleven and a half points from Wednesday's close to Thursday's settle at 28.18. SPX tried to rally twice. Both attempts failed. The index closed at 7,353.73 — a loss of 334.89 handles, 4.36%, the index's worst single session of the year — sitting near the session low. The Nasdaq shed 5.52%.

That close is the diagnostic, not the drawdown itself. When a session triggers two separate intraday recovery attempts that both fail to hold, and the final print sits near the low, the mechanical explanation is a regime where dealer hedging flows *with* price rather than against it. Each partial recovery generated a fresh layer of selling as dealers re-hedged their short gamma; each attempt to find a footing met an offer that wasn't from investors repositioning but from systematic flows that had no choice. That's what sustained negative-gamma cascade looks like on a session-by-session basis: the rallies don't hold because the entity that could absorb them is instead selling them.

## Friday's stall confirmed the vol wasn't done

**Friday was the monthly jobs report — a catalyst that often draws volatility back in.** SPX recovered 23.13 handles to 7,376.86, a gain of 0.31%. VIX pulled back only 2.54 points to 25.64 — still nearly nine points above where it had sat at Wednesday's close, still consistent with a regime where protection demand is elevated. A jobs print that moves the index three-tenths of a percent while VIX holds above 25 is not a floor being established. It's a stall with vol still priced for more.

## The read we keep

Event premium and structural gamma are two different sources of elevated vol. One has an expiration date. The other stays until the positioning unwinds — and an election result doesn't unwind positioning, it just removes one line item from the implied vol surface. The Wednesday compression was arithmetic. Thursday's cascade was structural.

We'll keep teaching you the pattern. We won't hand you the thresholds.
