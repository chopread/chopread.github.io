SPX entered the week at 7711.76 — Friday Aug 28's close — with VIX at 14.43. The first two sessions suggested something was building: Monday Aug 31 pushed the index down to 7686.14 with VIX nudging up to 14.92, and Tuesday Sep 1 extended that slide sharply. The index dropped to an intraday low of 7611.20 before closing at 7631.47 — down 54.67 points from Monday's close and 80.29 points below where the week had started. VIX spiked intraday to 16.80, closing at 16.34. Two down sessions, a 1.91-point VIX gain from Friday's close, and the week's low printed before Tuesday's bell.

Then the structure answered. Wednesday and Thursday combined to crush vol by 2.02 points and drive the index up 116.24 points close-to-close — from Tuesday's 7631.47 to Thursday's 7747.71. By Thursday's close, VIX sat at 14.32: not only lower than where Tuesday's spike had taken it, but lower than where the week had started. Friday gave back modest ground — SPX -0.38% to 7718.60, VIX +0.21 to 14.53 — and the week closed roughly flat: SPX up just 6.84 points net, VIX up 0.10 net. A fear episode that looked like it was gaining traction on Tuesday morning was entirely absorbed before Friday's open.

## Tuesday's spike and what it was pricing

**A 16.80 intraday VIX print after a two-day decline is the vol surface bidding for a fear premium that didn't get filled.** The Tuesday move had everything that tends to accompany the beginning of a sustained volatility regime: back-to-back down sessions building on each other, a sharp intraday breach to new weekly lows at 7611.20, and protection demand that pushed the near-term vol surface into the mid-16s within the first hours of trading. The market was pricing as if Wednesday would bring more of the same.

What reversed it was visible in Tuesday's own close. VIX finished at 16.34 — down from its 16.80 intraday high, even as SPX closed near its session low. When vol pulls back from its intraday spike while price is still at the lows, it signals that protection demand came in fast and got absorbed — not that sellers stepped away, but that dealers absorbing that demand found more natural supply on the other side than the spike implied. The surface decided it had overpriced the fear before the session ended. A VIX that can't hold its intraday high through the close is already telling you the ceiling.

## Wednesday and Thursday: the mechanical reversal

**The two-session unwind from Tuesday's close to Thursday's reads as mechanical rather than reactive.** On Wednesday, SPX gained 0.46% to 7666.60 while VIX dropped 1.14 points to 15.20 — the largest single-session VIX decline of the week. The recovery in price was real but modest; the recovery in vol surface was outsized relative to the index move. A 35-point SPX gain erasing 1.14 points of VIX isn't a one-for-one response. It's the signature of a market where dealers, having absorbed the Tuesday demand spike, are now running off exposure as the index stabilizes. When they no longer need the delta hedges built during Tuesday's down session, implied vol collapses faster than the index recovers.

Thursday sharpened the picture. SPX gained 1.06% to 7747.71 — the week's strongest session by a wide margin — while VIX dropped another 0.88 points to 14.32. A meaningful up day in price paired with a meaningful down day in vol is the signature of a tape where dealer gamma positioning is working in the structure's favor. When dealers are positioned net long gamma, upside price moves let them buy back short-delta hedges and reduce vol exposure simultaneously. The bigger the rally, the more mechanical the vol selling. By Thursday's close, VIX had unwound to 14.32 — 11 basis points below where the week had started — and the index had fully erased its Tuesday losses. The entire round trip: Tuesday's 16.80 intraday spike to Thursday's 14.32 close, in fewer than 48 hours of trading.

## The read we keep

Tuesday's VIX intraday print of 16.80 against an SPX low of 7611.20 looked like the setup for a wider breakdown. It wasn't. What followed — a 2.02-point vol crush across Wednesday and Thursday, an 81-point SPX recovery on Thursday alone, and a Friday that gave back a fraction of the gains without reigniting fear — was a vol spike meeting a structural absorption point rather than the leading edge of a regime change.

The 0.09% weekly SPX gain is not the story. The 48-hour round trip in VIX — from 14.43 to 16.80 intraday and back to 14.53 by Friday's close — is. When a vol surface absorbs a 2.37-point intraday spike and closes the week where it started, the spike was never a regime. It was inventory turnover. Learn to separate the two before you trade the fear.
